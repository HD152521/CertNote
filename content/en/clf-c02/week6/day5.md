# Day 5 - D-Day Wrap-Up: Exam Structure, Time Allocation, and a Final Check Checklist

At last, the final day. Rather than cramming in new knowledge, today we firm up the strategy for **how to take the exam**. Knowing the exam structure precisely, building a time-allocation plan, and holding an exam-day checklist in hand can earn you a higher score at the very same skill level.

## Exam Structure (CLF-C02)

| Item | Details |
|------|------|
| Number of questions | 65 questions |
| Time | 90 minutes |
| Scored questions | 50 questions (15 are unscored evaluation questions) |
| Question types | Multiple choice (one correct option), multiple response (two or more options) |
| Score range | 100–1,000 |
| Passing line | 700 |
| Delivery | Test center or online proctored |
| Results | Usually a pass/fail notification within a few business days |

> 💡 **Related theory**: Of the 65 questions, 15 are unscored evaluation questions. Since you can't tell which ones are evaluation questions, you must answer **every question seriously**. Passing is based on 700 out of a maximum 1,000.

## Time Allocation Strategy

- 65 questions / 90 minutes = about **1 minute 23 seconds** per question
- **First pass**: Go through all questions once within 60 minutes. Answer easy ones immediately; flag and skip the ones you're unsure about.
- **Second pass**: Use the remaining 30 minutes to re-examine the flagged questions.
- **Final check**: Confirm every question has an answer (a blank is always wrong, so fill it in even with a guess).

| Segment | Time | What to do |
|------|------|-------|
| 0–60 min | 60 min | First pass through all questions + flag |
| 60–85 min | 25 min | Re-examine flagged questions |
| 85–90 min | 5 min | Check for unanswered questions, fill in guesses |

> 💡 **Related theory**: A blank scores 0, but a guessed answer has a chance of being right. On a four-option question, eliminating two clearly wrong options raises your odds of being correct to 50%. Be sure to **fill in an answer for every question.**

- [ ] Rest well before the exam; avoid excessive last-minute cramming
- [ ] Arrive 30 minutes early (test center) or run a system pre-check (online)
- [ ] Submit only after answering every question

## Mindset

- Don't get rattled by one or two questions you don't know. Passing is 700 points, not a perfect score.
- Read phrases like "most appropriate / most cost-effective / least operational overhead" all the way through and grab the core keyword.
- Your first instinct is usually right. Don't change an answer without a reason.

**문제 3.** In managing exam time, what is the most correct strategy regarding a question left blank?

A) If unsure, leave it blank to avoid a penalty  
B) Eliminate clearly wrong options and always fill in an answer, even a guess  
C) Skip hard questions from the start and never look at them again  
D) Fill in C for every question uniformly  

**정답: B**  
해설: A blank always scores 0, but a guessed answer has a chance of being right, so after eliminating clearly wrong options you must fill in an answer. This exam has no penalty for wrong answers, so leaving a blank is disadvantageous, and permanently giving up on hard questions or blindly filling in one letter is not a good strategy either.

**문제 4.** When solving 65 questions in 90 minutes, which is the most appropriate recommended pacing strategy?

A) Stay on the first question until it's perfectly solved before moving on  
B) Handle easy questions quickly and flag hard ones to return to later  
C) Solve all questions at once in the final 10 minutes  
D) Solve the questions in random order  

**정답: B**  
해설: Handling easy questions quickly to bank time and flagging hard ones to re-examine on a second pass is how you maximize your score in limited time. Lingering on one question or cramming everything at the end leads to running out of time.
